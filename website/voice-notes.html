Voice

Or just say it.

Tap the microphone and talk the way you normally talk. What you said becomes part of the page you were already writing — same note, same place, ready to keep editing by hand.

Your voice lands in the note, not in a separate box.

There is no separate voice note to manage later. If your cursor is somewhere in the text, the transcript arrives right there. If you are just reading, it appends to the end. Then it is ordinary text you can edit, restructure, or add to by typing.

Punctuation, not rewriting.

Speech has sentences and pauses; writing shows them with punctuation and paragraph breaks. As Told adds that layer so what you said is readable — and stops there.

No grammar correction.

No paraphrasing.

No summarising.

No translating what you said.

Your slang, your names, your repetitions and your filler words stay in. The rule the whole feature is built on is simple: preserve the words, format the speech.

English, Telugu, Hindi — and the mix.

Most dictation quietly assumes one language per sentence. Real speech does not work that way, so As Told is built for code-switching and keeps each language in its own script rather than flattening everything into English.

What happens to the recording.

Turning speech into text needs more computing power than a phone should spend, so audio is sent over an encrypted connection to be transcribed and is not kept afterwards. Your note library itself stays on your device.
